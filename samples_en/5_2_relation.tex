The findings of this thesis are generally consistent with the literature reviewed
in Chapter~\ref{ch:rel_work}. Previous studies have shown that fine-tuning can
substantially improve the performance of code generation and translation models
by adapting them to a specific task or dataset. The improvements observed in
this thesis, particularly in terms of BLEU score, accepted solutions, and
compilation success, support this conclusion. Similarly, the stronger
performance of the Qwen models is consistent with recent benchmark studies,
which report that Qwen2.5-Coder achieves state-of-the-art or near
state-of-the-art performance on multiple programming benchmarks, including
HumanEval and MBPP \cite{qwen_coder}.

The experiments also support previous findings regarding the limitations of
BLEU as an evaluation metric for code-related tasks. %szb: lemaradtak a konkrétumok: previous findings konkrétan mit mondott erről, és te mit mondasz?
BLEU measures token overlap between the generated and reference outputs, but
multiple implementations of the same program can be functionally equivalent
while having very different textual representations. %szb: itt már nincs helye a bleu újbóli bevezetésének. Itt azt kell tárgyalni, hogy mások mit mondtak erről a témáról, és az hogyan viszonyul a te findingjaiddal
Earlier studies found that BLEU often correlates only weakly with semantic
correctness and human judgment in code translation tasks \cite{tran2019ruby,
evtikhiev2022bleu}. %szb: itt pl. le lehetne írni, hogy mások milyen korrelációt találtak (számszerűen, pontosan), és te mennyit. Szignifikáns a korreláció a statisztikai tesztek alapján? cikkek és te ugyanazt találtad?
Recent research therefore increasingly emphasizes execution-based evaluation,
where generated programs are compiled and tested against predefined test cases,
as a more reliable measure of functional correctness \cite{codescore2023,
ice_score}.
