\section{Objectives}

The primary objective of this thesis is to investigate the effectiveness of deep
learning-based approaches for automatic source code translation, with a
particular focus on the impact of model fine-tuning and evaluation
methodologies. While modern large language models achieve strong performance in
code-related tasks, it remains an open question to what extent fine-tuning can
further improve translation quality in terms of both syntactic accuracy and
functional correctness.

To address this, the thesis aims to analyze and compare the performance of base
and fine-tuned models on code translation tasks. In addition, multiple models
are evaluated under the same conditions in order to better understand their
strengths, limitations, and differences in behavior.

A key objective of this work is to examine the relationship between traditional
similarity-based evaluation metrics and execution-based evaluation methods.
Metrics such as BLEU are widely used due to their simplicity and efficiency, but
they do not necessarily reflect whether the generated code is correct from a
functional perspective \cite{bleu_metric}. Prior work has shown that similarity-
based metrics often correlate poorly with functional correctness, motivating the
use of execution-based evaluation methods \cite{evaluation_limitations,
huang2022exeds}. In this context, this thesis also investigates the relationship
between these evaluation approaches.

To achieve these goals, the thesis defines an experimental setup in which models
are trained and evaluated on a common dataset, using consistent preprocessing and
evaluation procedures. This ensures a fair comparison between models and between
base and fine-tuned variants.
